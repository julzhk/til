It's not the Reimann hypothesis, but conjuring up an app as an output channel in Claude is pretty clever. The AI platform consuming many of it's downstream products.

All would be very clear in a[ Wardley Maps](https://www.wardleymaps.com/) analysis I'd imagine: the platform moving closer to the consumer layer.

Actually the Wardley Mapping for AI would show the AI platforms just consume all of the products that rely on it, in that customer-facing consumer layer. Gen-AIs are so flexible and don't lend 
themselves to being build 'upon' like a regular building block or commodity, but somehow outflank these AI-wrappers and it's just easier to ask the AI directly for what you want.

I'm thinking about a spike web app project I made (when AI was new-ish and langchain was the thing to use... a distant year ago) for rehearsing for a job interview: it read the candidate's resume and 
the 
supplied job 
description. It  
used 
voice-synthesis to ask questions and transcribed 
verbal responses to ask the next round of questions. And graded your performance. Neat enough I thought..

Unsurprisingly, one response to a demo was: 'I just ask GPT for this stuff directly': turns out a text interface and asking ad hoc was better than my clever UX and user flow. It just wasn't 
adding enough value for a one-off/irregular task.

Now doing the fast work of building that app would be trivial: it's just a Claude artifact now.
