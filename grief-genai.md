TIL: The parallels between the phases of grief and the rate of change in the IT industry

I'm aware that Gen-AI is still in a relatively early stages
of the 'hype curve', so we're still at the over-reaction phase. If this is like all the other tech breakthroughs and bubbles, we'll get a more balanced opinion ... eventually.

Notwithstanding that caveat, it looks like a lot of developers (and now  mathematicians) are expressing their sadness at the sudden change in their working world. 'IT Engineer's have given up 
developing as the hard and interesting parts of the job have gone'

Many such examples: eg: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TUqScwjnO_k

It makes me think of the Kübler-Ross stages that describe the emotional journey of grief and other major losses.  Quoted below:

**The Five Stages:**

* **Denial**: A defense mechanism where you feel numb, shocked, or act as though the loss hasn't happened. It helps the mind pace the impact of the reality. 
* **Anger**: As denial fades, reality sets in, often turning into frustration, resentment, or asking "Why me?"—directed at yourself, others, or the situation. 
* **Bargaining**: An attempt to feel a sense of control or reverse the outcome by making mental "what-if" deals or promises (e.g., “If this is undone, I will change how I live”). 
* **Depression**: A deep sense of sadness, quiet despair, and exhaustion as the full weight and finality of the loss are felt. 
* **Acceptance**: Reaching a place of coming to terms with the reality of the loss. It doesn't mean you are "happy" or "over it," but rather that you are no longer fighting the reality and are learning to live with it.

Side note: it's often misunderstood to be linear phases. A checklist even. Maybe it shouldn't be called 'stages' and perhaps: 'aspects'.. anyway...

There were a lot of '**denial**' - Gen-ai is laughably limitted. 'How many R's in strawberries?'; and now lots of **depression**: 'My working life sucks etc'.

**Anger and bargaining**: This may be muted, as it's a bit unprofessional to vent these feelings. Nevertheless I'm sure I can find plenty of these emotions in 'Experienced Devs' sub-reddit.
 
**Acceptance**: We'll eventually collectively accept we're now some mix of: developer; code-factory-manager; customer and problem researcher; devops; FE, BE and on and on. So many more hats and 
responsibilities we'd 
previously defer to a 
colleague we now will be called on doing, and we'll accept this as the new normal.

We're two years ahead of the curve and these emotional Kübler-Ross 'aspects' are washing through the industry. It might be extremely painful when it consumes even larger components of the economy: 
a growing proportion of the working population under the gloomy cloud of 'work-grief'
